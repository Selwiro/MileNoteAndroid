Data conventions

01Data conventions

Four promises, written on this page and enforced in the code.

01

Local-first storage

Your ledger is a single JSON file in the app's private storage, written atomically. It never passes through a developer server.

02

No account needed

No phone number, no email, no third-party login. Open the app and go. No subscriptions, no ads either.

03

Transparent full-tank method

Only the distance between two full tanks counts, and a missed fill-up resets the baseline. Overall economy is weighted by distance — never guessed, never padded.

04

All data travels with you

A complete JSON backup holds every vehicle and custom item, in a format shared with the web version; expenses export to CSV.

02Why MileNote

Not another expense tracker —
a ledger for one car.

Only car-related spending, with every rule spelled out: when a figure can't be computed it shows “—” instead of a guess.

A ledger should look like a ledger: categories, totals, and a clear trail.

01

One car, one ledger

Add as many vehicles as you like; expenses, fuel economy and maintenance stay separated per vehicle. With two family cars, fuel and service costs never blur together.

A complete JSON backup still covers every vehicle.

02

Full-tank fuel economy

The first full tank sets the baseline, partial fills carry over to the next settlement, and marking a missed fill-up resets the baseline. Overall economy is weighted by distance, not a simple average.

Records with a decreasing or missing odometer are skipped.

03

Service due, whichever comes first

12 default service plans move to your to-do list when either the distance or the date falls due. Completing one records it and schedules the next round; after the first service, dates and mileage are recalculated from the actual entry.

Due items only show up in the app — there are no system notifications.

05Feature list

Everything one car's books need

It has what it should, and sends nothing it shouldn't — all calculations and scanning happen on device.

Nine categories + custom ones

Fuel, insurance, maintenance, repairs, detailing, tolls, parking, fines and car supplies; add your own categories and they join filters, charts and import matching.

Add entry

The category icon grid sits on top; amount, date, odometer and fuel fields fit on one screen. The amount field can scan a receipt, and you confirm before saving.

Receipt scanning

Take a photo with CameraX or pick one from the gallery; on-device ML Kit reads the amount paid, fuel volume and date. Parsing stays conservative — discounts and unit prices never become expenses.

Four import paths

Bear (XLS/XLSX), Qianji, a maintenance log and JSON backups; with field mapping, row-by-row preview and automatic duplicate skipping, plus importing straight from the system share sheet.

Analytics

Stacked monthly bars show spending trends and a donut shows cost composition; tap a bar segment or slice for the category, amount and share. Charts and records live on two tabs.

Fuel economy

Measured between full tanks and weighted by distance: economy trend, average fuel cost, cost per kilometre, and today's fuel price with the next adjustment date and direction.

Service plan

12 default plans in a table — date on top, mileage below, sorted by how soon they fall due; completing one records it, and any single reminder can be exported as an .ics calendar file.

Backup & export

Complete JSON backup and restore, CSV export for the current vehicle, and optional WebDAV (Nutstore/Jianguoyun, Nextcloud, Synology) with the password encrypted on device; sync runs in the foreground only — upload or download by hand, or turn on foreground auto-sync.

Native experience

Kotlin + Jetpack Compose + Material 3 Expressive; Photo Picker, SAF document picking, system share intake and coarse location — just four permissions.

06Data & privacy

The ledger belongs to you,
no server required.

No account system, no cloud database, no analytics. Only a WebDAV server you configure yourself will ever sync your ledger, to a location you choose.

Local-first

The ledger lives in a single JSON file in the app's private storage, with vehicle photos embedded as data URLs and writes done atomically. Nothing passes through a developer server.

No account

No phone number, email or third-party login — just open the app. No subscriptions and no ads either.

Take it with you

A complete JSON backup includes every vehicle, record, reminder and custom item, in a format shared with the web version; expenses export to CSV.

WebDAV optional

Sync to a WebDAV server you provide; the connection password is encrypted with Android Keystore + AES-256-GCM. Sync runs in the foreground only — upload or download by hand, or turn on foreground auto-sync; leaving the app ends it.

08FAQ

Questions you might have

Do I need an account? Is my data uploaded?

No. The ledger stays on your device by default and never passes through a developer server; it is only synced when you configure WebDAV in Settings, and then only to the server you enter.

How do I move to a new phone?

Export a complete JSON backup on the old device and restore it on the new one. The format matches the web version (version: 2) and web → Android restores have been tested; you can also upload or download manually through WebDAV.

Why is fuel economy different from the dashboard readout?

The full-tank method uses the actual fuel added and the distance between two full tanks, which differs from the instantaneous or long-term figures a trip computer shows; road conditions, air conditioning, load and tyre pressure all affect the result. This tool is only responsible for the data you enter.

Should maintenance follow mileage or time?

Whichever comes first: when either the distance or the date falls due, the plan moves to your to-do list. Plans that only track distance never estimate a date; everything is managed in the service plan table.

Does it support electric cars?

You can record charging, service and insurance with custom categories; full-tank economy and today's fuel price target petrol cars, and a pure EV has no energy-consumption model.

Why are there no system notifications?

A deliberate trade-off: no background service and no notification permission. Due maintenance appears in the in-app car to-do list and in the service plan; when needed, export an .ics file to your system calendar.

Can I lose my data?

Writes are atomic, but uninstalling clears the app's private storage — export a complete JSON backup before uninstalling, switching phones or clearing data. With WebDAV configured you can also restore from the remote copy.
